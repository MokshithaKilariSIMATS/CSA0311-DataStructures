WHY DIJKSTRA + MIN-HEAP

Routing algorithm

Roads carry positive, dynamically-changing weights. That combination — weighted, non-negative, frequently updated — is exactly the case Dijkstra's algorithm with a binary min-heap is built for.

Why not the alternatives

BFS finds the fewest hops, not the shortest weighted distance — with road lengths and traffic penalties differing per edge, a low hop-count route can easily be the slower one.

Floyd-Warshall solves all-pairs shortest paths in O(V³), which recomputes routes nobody asked for. For single dispatch queries on a sparse ~130-node city graph that's wasted work; Dijkstra answers one query in O((V+E) log V).

Adjacency lists only store edges that exist. On a sparse graph like this one (each intersection touches roughly 4 roads, not 129), a matrix would allocate 129× more cells than needed.
